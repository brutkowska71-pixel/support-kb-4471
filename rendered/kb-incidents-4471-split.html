#4471 · three problems, not one

owner Beata Rutkowska open reviewed 2026-11-07 · 0 days ago

#4471 says every report about a wrong date is one bug: the parser reads dates month first. The reports say otherwise. Read by tenant and by what each report describes, #4471 holds three problems with three different fixes. The queued parser fix closes only the first one.

#4471 is not known. Split it: one ticket per problem below. Do not close #4471's reports when the parser fix ships; the other two problems will keep sending them.

The counts come from reports-4471.json at render time. The export repeats some reports across its pages; each id counts once.

parser: the shared parser reads day-first dates month first

A day-first tenant books 7 April (07/04); the parser reads 4 July. Every channel shows it: the app, email, and the booking-v2 form, whose hint already says DD/MM/YYYY.

  • R-41200 · harbour.dental · app — “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July. Is this a joke. It is not April Fools where we are.”
  • R-41201 · harbour.dental · app — “Sorry. 4 July went out on 7 April. I have been up since five.”
  • R-41203 · lantern.row · app — “Booked for 2 December. The invite says 12 February.”

371 more like these in the export.

Fix: parse with the tenant’s locale (dayFirst() in causes.ts already knows it). This is the fix #4471 queued.

hint: booking-v3 tells day-first tenants to type month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. The tenant’s people type what the hint says, month first, and the form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The date flips before the parser sees it. Only reports that came in through the form, from day-first tenants on booking-v3, have this cause.

  • R-41230 · copper.studio · form — “My client came on 1 June instead of 6 January. We sorted it out.”
  • R-41241 · alder.physio · form — “We had two people show up on 8 June. They were booked for 6 August.”
  • R-41251 · pale.harbour · form — “Wednesday 7 January showed as Wednesday 1 July. I moved it myself.”

30 more like these in the export.

Fix: print the tenant’s own date order under the field (or use a date picker). The parser fix does not touch this: these dates are already wrong when the form submits them.

shift: the date moves by one day

These reports do not describe a flipped day and month. The date moves by one day, or the same meeting shows a different day to people in different time zones. A flipped date cannot do that. The desk noticed it and listed them (desk-notes.json), but #4471 still counts them as parser.

  • R-41202 · kettle.books · email — “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May. My client turned up on the Monday. We had coffee.”
  • R-41209 · alder.physio · app — “Every booking in June is one day off. Only June. Why only June.”
  • R-41236 · nine.lanterns · app — “The invite arrived before I made the booking. I would like to know how.”
  • R-41344 · pale.harbour · email — “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting. We held it twice.”
  • R-41620 · lantern.row · email — “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday. I moved it myself. I have stopped reporting these but my colleague says it helps you.”

Fix: look at time zones: how the invite’s date is stored and sent (date-only values turned into midnight UTC, or a time zone or DST rule applied twice). Neither date-order fix touches it.

How they ended up in one ticket

Decision 0001 merges reports by subject line. All three problems produce the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Month and day swapped?”), so they landed in one ticket. Intake’s tag does not separate them either: it guesses from the reporter’s browser, not the tenant.
